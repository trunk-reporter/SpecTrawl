## Hardware

- **capture_channel drops samples at 5 MS/s cf32 output** (seen 2026-10-11: the 751 MHz LTE capture came out 32768 samples short with no warning; the example's ring follow-up falls behind). Warn on a ring overrun and lower the default output rate.

- **Manual Airspy gain stages** (done 2026-10-11): Gain → Manual gives LNA, mixer and VGA sliders (AGC off) through `GainSetting::Manual`; the hop profile is keyed by it. Untested on hardware tonight.
- **tinySA Ultra support** (on hand). A swept spectrum analyzer, not an SDR: no IQ, so no look-back or decoding. Three uses:
  - **Calibration reference:** reads in dBm, so measuring the same signal on both gives per-device dBFS→dBm offsets for the Airspys.
  - **Test source:** its signal-generator output gives known tones to measure Airspy gain tables and passband shape, and to test detection repeatably.
  - **Survey outside the R2's range:** about 100 kHz–6 GHz (HF below 24 MHz; 1.8–6 GHz for cellular, Wi-Fi, drone links).
  - Needs a second kind of source, a *power-sweep device* producing spectrum rows directly (USB serial text protocol). The Survey role and burst detector already work on rows. Check its COM port and firmware commands before writing it.
- **Antenna multicoupler** (on hand: Stridsberg MCA204M, BNC, 25 MHz–1 GHz; measured 2026-11-03 on a NanoVNA V2). Gain +3 to +4 dB from 50–470 MHz, about −1.5 to +0.5 dB at 806–940 MHz, outputs within ~1 dB of each other. The splitter is two pairs (1–2, 3–4): isolation within a pair 21–23 dB at VHF/UHF but only 15–17 dB at 806–940 MHz; across pairs (1→3) 27–28 dB at VHF/UHF and 22–23 dB at 806–940. Put the two R2s on different pairs. Terminated-input noise floor through it (R2 at sensitivity 0.8): ~1–1.5 dB worse SNR at VHF/UHF, ~2–2.5 dB at 806–940 MHz. With its input terminated, local carriers still leak in (cellular 600/700/850, TV ch 16, 6–16 dB over the floor), partly through the SMA jumper and adapter, partly at the coupler or its 12 V lead: try a ferrite on that lead and better-shielded jumpers. Terminated-input spur maps must use a terminator directly on the radio's SMA (clean). Left: on-air check of the noise cost and of the R2 LO leakage through it. One antenna feeding every receiver makes them comparable:
  - **Uses:** A/B acceptance of new radios (e.g. a HackRF Pro clone against an Airspy on identical input); per-device frequency error and level offsets in one measurement, with a tinySA tone injected once reaching every receiver; and a cross-receiver spur test (with matched gains, a line one radio sees and the others don't is internal), stronger than the spacing heuristics in `engine_old::emitters::spur_context`.
  - **Active, with a buffer amplifier per output:** ≥ 40 dB port-to-port isolation. A passive splitter (~20–25 dB) would carry each R2's LO into the other and make the LO leakage below worse.
  - **Linearity before gain:** the 33 dB repeater at 855.6375, FM broadcast and LTE are strong locally; a cheap amplifier's intermodulation would make false signals. Check OIP3; no amplified TV splitters.
  - **Coverage** at least 24 MHz–1.8 GHz (the Airspys). A HackRF can keep its own wideband antenna or a separate high-band path.
  - **Ports matched within ~1 dB**, roughly unity gain overall and a low noise figure, so splitting costs the Airspys no sensitivity.
- **R2s hear each other's local oscillator** (partly done 2026-10-11): the survey blanks each focus Airspy's centre + 5 MHz ± 15 kHz as it retunes (`Source::set_masks`), besides hiding survey detections inside focus spans. Left: the weaker spur offsets (measure them), and the attenuation/filter suggestion. On the MCA204M (outputs 1 and 3, other pairs) the focus LO was not visible in the survey at 230, 410, 820 or 945 MHz (2026-11-03, focus streamed at W−4 and W−3.5 MHz, nothing >6 dB that moved with it).
- **Two radios on one USB bus interfere.** With both R2s sharing a hub, the sweeping one put a dense comb of narrow carriers into the other (856.6–859.5 MHz). On separate buses it's gone. 2026-11-03, both on hub 3-2 through the multicoupler: whenever the focus R2 streamed (whatever its tuning), the survey showed a line at its own centre (820.002 MHz, +22 dB) and lines 3–4 MHz off centre at 230 MHz (+8 dB); all gone when the focus stopped. It also corrupts the samples in bursts: two R2s streaming on bus 3 through one antenna decoded no P25, FLEX or DCS at all, where one alone (same build, same antenna, same minutes) decoded 254 P25, both FLEX channels and DCS 205; the smeared rows put 13–26 dB pedestals around strong signals and fused adjacent site channels into 60–350 kHz records. The cause (measured 2026-11-04, R2 + HydraSDR, `rateprobe` counting samples per second): the shared USB 2 link carries about 22.5 MB/s, so two radios at 10 MS/s (20 MB/s each, 15 packed) get 5.6 MS/s each (7.5 packed); the rest overflows the boards' FIFOs and vanishes without libairspy or libhydrasdr reporting a drop. Done: the backend counts delivered samples against the clock over 2 s windows and reports a shortfall over 5% as a gap (keeping sample time true to wall time); the app's pane titles say "LOSING n% OF SAMPLES" (and whether another radio shares the controller, `backend_airspy::usb_bus`); the burst detector no longer lets smeared rows widen or fuse records (below). On eddie every USB-A port is behind the onboard hub (3-2); only the USB-C ports are on the other controller. Left: a note in docs; a second radio at full rate needs its own controller (or one radio at 2.5 MS/s, untested).
- **A second Airspy sometimes can't be opened while the first streams** (`AIRSPY_ERROR_NOT_FOUND`), seen in the `classify_sim` example but not in the app. One trigger found 2026-11-04: another process enumerating holds each board for a moment, so an open at the same instant misses it; opens now retry four times 250 ms apart.
